# 1. Executive Summary

This document defines the recommended sales deck and company profile structure for AICORE Technologies Limited. It is designed to help AICORE present itself professionally to prospects, partners, schools, energy companies, SMEs, institutions, startups, and organizations that may need AI, automation, embedded systems, IoT, robotics, SaaS, mobile applications, developer tools, technical training, or intelligent systems.

The goal is to create a reusable market-facing PDF/pitch document that introduces AICORE clearly, explains what the company builds, shows where AICORE can help, presents relevant service pillars and products, and guides the recipient toward a consultation, demo, training discussion, partnership conversation, or project scoping meeting.

The sales deck/company profile should not present AICORE as a generic website development, design, or UI/UX agency. It should present AICORE as:

> **An AI, automation, and intelligent systems company that builds practical technology systems across software, AI, embedded firmware, IoT, robotics, SaaS platforms, mobile applications, developer tools, and technical training.**

The central brand idea remains:

> **AI • Automation • Intelligent Systems**

The strongest business message is:

> **AICORE connects AI, software, automation, embedded devices, IoT, robotics, dashboards, mobile applications, and technical training into practical systems that solve real business and operational problems.**

This company profile should be designed as a polished PDF that can be sent by email, attached to proposals, shared after meetings, printed for partners, adapted into a presentation deck, and later generated or personalized through AICORE GrowthOS.

The document should work for multiple audiences, but it must remain focused. It should communicate:

* who AICORE is;
* what AICORE builds;
* why AICORE is different;
* the industries AICORE serves;
* the service pillars AICORE offers;
* the products and platforms AICORE is building;
* how AICORE works with clients;
* what kind of projects AICORE can handle;
* what proof/case studies should be shown;
* how prospects can start a conversation.

The recommended main deck/profile structure is:

1. Cover page
2. Opening positioning statement
3. About AICORE
4. The problem AICORE solves
5. What makes AICORE different
6. Core service pillars
7. Products and platforms
8. Industry use-case matrix
9. Audience-specific solution pages
10. AICORE Academy section
11. Case studies and portfolio approach
12. Delivery approach
13. Engagement packages
14. Technology capability stack
15. Partnership opportunities
16. Why work with AICORE
17. Call to action and contact page

The final designed document should feel like a technical company profile and pitch document, not a generic agency brochure. It should use clean layouts, strong headings, technical diagrams, product/platform previews, industry cards, service icons, selected screenshots/mockups, and simple system flows.

# 2. Purpose of the Sales Deck / Company Profile

## 2.1 Primary Purpose

The primary purpose of the AICORE Sales Deck / Company Profile is to help AICORE introduce itself to potential customers and partners in a structured, professional, and technically credible way.

It should help AICORE answer five questions quickly:

1. Who is AICORE?
2. What does AICORE build?
3. Which problems does AICORE solve?
4. Which industries and organizations can AICORE serve?
5. What is the next step for a prospect, partner, school, or institution?

## 2.2 Business Purpose

The document should support:

* prospect outreach;
* sales meetings;
* partnership discussions;
* school/institution proposals;
* energy and IoT project conversations;
* SME automation conversations;
* product demo requests;
* training programme introductions;
* proposal attachments;
* post-meeting follow-up;
* event/networking introductions;
* downloadable website profile material.

## 2.3 Strategic Role

The sales deck/company profile should make AICORE look more serious, more technical, and more product-oriented than a normal software agency.

It should shift perception from:

> “A company that builds websites or apps.”

To:

> “A technology engineering company that builds AI-powered software, automation platforms, embedded systems, IoT solutions, robotics systems, SaaS products, mobile apps, and technical training programmes.”

## 2.4 What This Document Should Help AICORE Sell

The profile should help sell:

* AI business tools;
* business process automation;
* operational dashboards;
* SaaS platforms;
* mobile applications;
* embedded firmware;
* Raspberry Pi / BeagleBone / ESP32 systems;
* IoT monitoring platforms;
* smart energy monitoring;
* device fleet management;
* edge AI and computer vision systems;
* robotics/STEM training;
* AICORE Academy programmes;
* product prototypes and MVPs;
* technical partnerships;
* sector-specific intelligent systems.

## 2.5 What This Document Should Avoid

The profile should avoid:

* presenting AICORE as a website design agency;
* leading with generic UI/UX or graphic design;
* overusing “digital transformation” without explaining real systems;
* listing too many random services without structure;
* using vague AI language;
* showing only screenshots without workflow explanation;
* making unsupported claims;
* promising product readiness where a product is still a concept, prototype, or MVP;
* sounding like a generic IT services brochure.

# 4. Document Format and Design Direction

## 4.1 Recommended Format

The main output should be a designed PDF that can also be adapted into a slide deck.

Recommended formats:

* PDF profile for email sharing;
* 16:9 presentation deck for live pitching;
* A4 printable company profile;
* short 6-page teaser version;
* long 20-page capability statement;
* one-page company summary.

## 4.2 Recommended Visual Style

The visual style should follow AICORE’s website visual direction:

> **Clean technical interface + engineering credibility + system diagrams + product screenshots + restrained futuristic accents.**

The deck/profile should feel:

* technical;
* modern;
* clean;
* structured;
* credible;
* engineering-led;
* business-focused;
* product-aware.

It should not feel:

* like a generic agency brochure;
* overly decorative;
* too text-heavy;
* like a cheap template;
* like an abstract AI hype presentation.

## 4.3 Suggested Color Direction

Use the approved visual color direction:

| Role               | Suggested Color | Usage                                                  |
| ------------------ | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Deep Navy          | `#07111F`       | Cover page, section dividers, dark technical pages.    |
| AICORE Blue        | `#1E5BFF`       | Primary headings, CTAs, icons, key highlights.         |
| Signal Cyan        | `#00D4FF`       | AI/data accents, connector lines, diagrams.            |
| Automation Teal    | `#00B894`       | Automation, IoT, success, monitoring visuals.          |
| Smart Energy Amber | `#F59E0B`       | Energy, alerts, Academy highlights, selected callouts. |
| Cool White         | `#F8FAFC`       | Light section backgrounds.                             |
| Ink                | `#111827`       | Primary text.                                          |
| Slate              | `#64748B`       | Supporting text.                                       |

## 4.4 Typography Direction

Recommended fonts:

* Headings: Sora, Space Grotesk, Manrope, or Inter;
* Body: Inter or Manrope;
* Technical labels: JetBrains Mono or IBM Plex Mono.

Rules:

* Use bold, short headlines.
* Keep paragraphs short.
* Use large readable text for presentation pages.
* Use tables sparingly and only where they improve clarity.
* Use icon-led cards instead of long bullet lists where possible.

## 4.5 Design Components

The deck/profile should use:

* section divider pages;
* service cards;
* product cards;
* industry cards;
* system diagrams;
* workflow flows;
* technology stack strips;
* CTA panels;
* case study cards;
* audience-specific inserts;
* contact/footer blocks.
