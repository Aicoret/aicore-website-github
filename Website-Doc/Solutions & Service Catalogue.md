# 1. Executive Summary

AICORE Technologies Limited should present its services as a structured catalogue of intelligent technology solutions rather than as a generic list of website, software, or design services. The company’s strongest market position is its ability to combine AI, automation, software engineering, embedded firmware, IoT, robotics, dashboards, cloud systems, mobile applications, and technical training into complete working systems.

This Solutions & Service Catalogue defines AICORE’s major service pillars, the specific offerings under each pillar, the problems each service solves, common use cases, expected deliverables, likely technologies, target customers, qualification questions, proposal notes, and GrowthOS matching rules.

The recommended service architecture is organized into eight main pillars:

1. AI Systems and AI Tools
2. Process and Business Automation
3. Intelligent Platforms and SaaS Products
4. Embedded, IoT and Edge AI Systems
5. Robotics and Intelligent Hardware
6. Developer Tools and Engineering Utilities
7. Mobile and Cross-Platform Applications
8. AICORE Academy and Technical Training

The purpose of this catalogue is not only marketing. It should also become the internal reference for:

* public website service pages;
* proposal writing;
* service package creation;
* lead qualification;
* GrowthOS AI service matching;
* outreach personalization;
* solution scoping;
* product roadmap planning;
* content marketing themes;
* training programme positioning.

The catalogue follows the brand direction:

> **AI • Automation • Intelligent Systems**

The key message is:

> **AICORE builds practical intelligent systems that connect software, AI, automation, hardware, data, workflows, dashboards, and people.**

# 2. Catalogue Positioning

## 2.1 Catalogue Purpose

This catalogue exists to clearly define what AICORE can offer to clients and how those services should be described, sold, scoped, delivered, and matched to prospects.

It answers:

* What service pillars should AICORE promote?
* What specific offerings belong under each pillar?
* What business problems does each offering solve?
* Which customers are the best fit?
* What deliverables should AICORE provide?
* What technologies may be used?
* What questions should be asked before scoping?
* What should GrowthOS use when matching leads to services?
* Which services are MVP-ready and which are advanced or future-facing?

## 2.2 Strategic Role of the Catalogue

The service catalogue should help AICORE:

* avoid weak generic service positioning;
* focus on higher-value AI, automation, embedded, IoT, SaaS, and intelligent systems work;
* make proposals easier to prepare;
* help GrowthOS recommend relevant services to leads;
* support sector-specific outreach;
* improve consistency between website copy, sales conversations, proposals, and delivery;
* make service packaging clearer;
* define what AICORE should say “yes” or “no” to.

## 2.3 What This Catalogue Is Not

This catalogue is not:

* a generic agency service list;
* a pricing sheet;
* a final contract template;
* a detailed technical architecture document for every service;
* a promise that AICORE will accept every type of project;
* a list of low-value website or graphic design services.

Instead, it is a strategic service system that presents AICORE as an intelligent systems company.

# 3. Service Positioning Rules

## 3.1 Main Service Positioning Statement

> **AICORE designs and builds AI-powered software, automation systems, embedded firmware, IoT platforms, robotics prototypes, SaaS products, developer tools, mobile applications, and technical training programmes for organizations that want to operate smarter.**

## 3.2 Service Catalogue Summary Statement

> **Our solutions help organizations automate workflows, connect devices, monitor assets, deploy AI into operations, build scalable platforms, and train teams for modern technology environments.**

## 3.3 How to Present Web and UI Work

AICORE can still build web applications, dashboards, admin portals, and frontend interfaces. However, this should be framed as part of larger systems.

Do not present AICORE as:

* a website design company;
* a graphics design company;
* a generic UI/UX agency;
* a low-cost web development vendor.

Use:

> **We build web-based platforms, dashboards, SaaS products, and operational interfaces for AI, automation, IoT, and business systems.**

Use:

> **We design practical interfaces for complex systems, dashboards, mobile apps, automation workflows, and intelligent platforms.**

## 3.4 Service Messaging Principles

Every service description should explain:

1. What AICORE builds.
2. What operational or business problem it solves.
3. Who it is for.
4. What deliverables the customer receives.
5. What technologies may be involved.
6. How the service can grow after the first phase.

## 3.5 Service Maturity Levels

Use the following maturity labels internally:

| Level              | Meaning                                                            |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Core Offering      | AICORE should actively promote and deliver this now.               |
| Strategic Offering | AICORE should promote selectively where the lead has a strong fit. |
| Advanced Offering  | Requires careful scoping, higher budget, or technical validation.  |
| Future Offering    | Should be mentioned as roadmap/future capability, not oversold.    |

## 18.5 Matching Guardrails

GrowthOS should:

* only recommend active service catalogue items;
* include confidence level;
* include reasoning;
* identify missing information;
* avoid unsupported assumptions;
* avoid recommending hardware-heavy services without evidence of hardware/monitoring need;
* avoid recommending AI unless there is a clear workflow or knowledge task;
* allow technical lead review for complex services.

# 20. Service Catalogue Governance

## 20.1 Ownership

The service catalogue should be owned by AICORE management and reviewed by both the technical and business development teams.

## 20.2 Review Frequency

Review the catalogue:

* monthly during early GrowthOS development;
* quarterly after public website launch;
* whenever a major service is added or removed;
* after significant client feedback;
* after a successful project that should become a repeatable offer.

## 20.3 Change Rules

Any update should specify:

* what changed;
* why it changed;
* which service category is affected;
* whether website copy must change;
* whether GrowthOS prompts must change;
* whether proposal templates must change;
* whether pricing assumptions must change.

## 20.4 Catalogue Quality Rules

Each service item must have:

* clear name;
* clear category;
* target customer;
* business problem solved;
* use cases;
* deliverables;
* technologies;
* qualification questions;
* proposal notes;
* maturity level;
* active/inactive status.

AICORE should avoid making these standalone public services:

* generic website design;
* generic UI/UX design;
* logo design;
* graphics design;
* social media flyer design;
* cheap website packages;
* basic IT support;
* general software outsourcing with no strategic fit.

If a client asks for a website, reposition the conversation:

> **Do you need a simple website, or do you need a platform, dashboard, customer portal, automation system, or lead-capture engine?**

If the requirement is only a low-budget brochure website with no strategic value, AICORE should consider declining or packaging it only as part of a broader system.
