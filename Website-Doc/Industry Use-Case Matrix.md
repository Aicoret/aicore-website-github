# 2. Purpose of the Industry Use-Case Matrix

## 2.1 Document Purpose

The purpose of this document is to connect AICORE’s capabilities to real industry needs. A service catalogue explains what AICORE can build. A product catalogue explains what AICORE owns or is developing. This Industry Use-Case Matrix explains **where those capabilities matter most** and **how they should be positioned for different sectors**.

It defines:

* target industries;
* sector-specific operational problems;
* relevant AICORE service pillars;
* relevant AICORE products and platforms;
* sample use cases;
* likely decision makers;
* discovery questions;
* proposal angles;
* website copy;
* recommended CTAs;
* GrowthOS classification and matching tags;
* sector prioritization logic.

## 2.2 Why This Document Is Important

AICORE has many technical capabilities. Without an industry matrix, those capabilities may appear as a long technical list. A potential client may not immediately understand how AI, firmware, IoT, SaaS, robotics, mobile apps, and automation apply to their own business.

The matrix solves this by translating AICORE’s capabilities into industry-specific language.

For example:

* A school may not ask for “AI systems and embedded platforms,” but it may need student support tools, robotics training, digital registration, practical STEM kits, and learning dashboards.
* A warehouse may not ask for “intelligent platforms,” but it may need inventory traceability, receiving verification, inspection gates, dispatch control, and barcode/RFID integration.
* An energy company may not ask for “IoT gateways,” but it may need solar monitoring, transformer status visibility, battery alerts, inverter monitoring, and device fleet administration.
* A clinic may not ask for “process automation,” but it may need appointment workflows, laboratory sample tracking, document processing, customer communication, and operational dashboards.

This document helps AICORE communicate with each sector in the language of the sector, while still maintaining the company’s core identity: **AI • Automation • Intelligent Systems**.

# 4. Industry Prioritization Framework

## 4.1 Prioritization Criteria

AICORE should prioritize industries using the following criteria:

| Criterion           | Meaning                                                                                                      |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Pain Urgency        | How serious or frequent the industry problem is.                                                             |
| Budget Potential    | Whether the sector can realistically pay for AICORE’s work.                                                  |
| Technical Fit       | How strongly the sector matches AICORE’s AI, automation, embedded, IoT, software, and training capabilities. |
| Product Fit         | Whether AICORE already has a relevant product/platform concept.                                              |
| Demo Potential      | Whether the sector can be served with visible demos, screenshots, or prototypes.                             |
| Repeatability       | Whether solutions can be reused across multiple customers.                                                   |
| Sales Accessibility | Whether AICORE can reach decision makers through direct outreach, referrals, content, or partnerships.       |
| Case Study Value    | Whether successful work in the sector would strengthen AICORE’s public credibility.                          |

## 4.2 Recommended Industry Priority Levels

| Priority   | Industries                                                                                | Reason                                                                                                         |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Priority 1 | SMEs, schools/training institutions, energy/utilities, logistics/warehousing              | Strong fit with existing service pillars and product concepts; clear website messaging; strong demo potential. |
| Priority 2 | Agriculture/agro-processing, manufacturing, security/surveillance, real estate/facilities | Strong IoT, monitoring, automation, and edge AI potential; may require more careful technical scoping.         |
| Priority 3 | Healthcare clinics/labs, retail/e-commerce, professional services, startups/innovators    | Good AI/software/automation fit; may need more tailored positioning and compliance awareness.                  |
| Priority 4 | Government/public sector, hospitality/service businesses                                  | Useful but may require longer sales cycles, procurement complexity, or more careful proposal preparation.      |

## 4.3 Recommended Early Outreach Focus

For the first AICORE website and GrowthOS outreach campaigns, prioritize:

1. SMEs and growing businesses — AI tools, automation, internal systems, GrowthOS-style workflows.
2. Schools and training institutions — Academy, robotics/STEM, AI learning tools, registration systems.
3. Energy and utilities — smart energy monitoring, IoT monitoring, device fleet systems.
4. Logistics and warehousing — Throughport WMS, traceability, inventory visibility, verification workflows.
5. Agriculture and agro-processing — sensor monitoring, cold chain, storage monitoring, process automation.

* Scattered customer records.
* Repeated customer enquiries.
* Weak reporting and visibility.
* Manual approvals and internal task tracking.
* Poor documentation and knowledge sharing.
* Lack of simple dashboards for business operations.
* No structured way to use AI in daily work.

# 12. Proposal and Discovery Usage

## 12.1 Proposal Structure for Industry-Based Opportunities

For industry-specific proposals, use this structure:

1. Cover page
2. Executive summary
3. Industry context
4. Client problem statement
5. Proposed AICORE solution
6. Use-case mapping
7. System workflow
8. Modules and deliverables
9. Technology approach
10. Deployment plan
11. Training and support
12. Future improvement path
13. Budget or pricing assumptions
14. Conclusion

## 12.2 Industry Discovery Checklist

Before preparing a proposal, ask:

* What industry does the client operate in?
* What current workflow is manual, slow, risky, or disconnected?
* Which users are involved?
* What data is currently collected?
* What devices, systems, or tools already exist?
* What must be monitored, automated, or reported?
* What is the minimum useful first phase?
* Does the client need software only, hardware integration, or both?
* Does the client need AI assistance, dashboards, alerts, mobile access, or training?
* Who approves the project internally?
* What success outcome should be visible after deployment?

## 12.3 Recommended First-Phase Scoping Rule

For most industries, AICORE should avoid proposing a very large system immediately. Start with a pilot or MVP that proves the core workflow.

Examples:

* For energy: monitor 3–5 assets first.
* For warehouses: digitize receiving and item traceability first.
* For schools: run one robotics/STEM programme first.
* For SMEs: automate one workflow first.
* For clinics: automate one admin workflow first.
* For manufacturing: monitor one machine or production line first.
